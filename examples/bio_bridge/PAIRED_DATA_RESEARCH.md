# Public paired fly data for future neural–embedding work

Audit date: 2026-09-17. Recommendation: begin with a small **real neural-response calibration** dataset and a separate **FlyVis synthetic temporal benchmark**. For actual brain–behavior alignment, prioritize the Aimon regional time series or the descending-neuron recordings after confirming their per-animal files and synchronization. No biological alignment model was trained or implemented in this audit.

Static connectomes give anatomical wiring, not synchronized activity, stimulus, or behavior. FlyVis and FlyGym can generate matched simulated signals; real calcium/voltage recordings require measurement models and timing correction. None of the resources below supplies a fly's natural-language thoughts. Text embeddings can describe experimental stimuli or operational behavior annotations, with that interpretation stated explicitly.

## Concrete schema evidence and import boundaries

**FlyVis:** the tutorial's `BoxEye` takes `[sequence, frame, height, width]` and emits `[sequence, frame, 1, 721]`; its `datamate` example writes `sequences.h5`. `random_walk_of_blocks` avoids downloading an image corpus. Store stimulus seed, full sequence ID, frame time, contrast/motion parameters, model/ensemble member, and neuron/type coordinates in our eventual manifest. Do not interpret an ensemble member as an animal. The model was task-optimized; it should not be described as trained directly on all recorded neuron responses. [Author tutorial](https://turagalab.github.io/flyvis/examples/07_flyvision_providing_custom_stimuli/), [official implementation](https://github.com/TuragaLab/flyvis).

**Aimon:** CRCNS recordings last roughly one or two minutes, with about five flies per condition; imaging modality and frame rates differ. [CRCNS description](https://crcns.org/data-sets/ia/fly-1/about-fly-1). In the newer pinned repository, notebook source explicitly contains `FlyID`, `expID`, `GAL4`, `FR`, `WalkRegressor`, `TurnRegressor` and condition-specific time bounds. It loads MATLAB `TS` regional traces and walk/turn regressors. These are useful synchronization/grouping clues, not a validated portable schema: paths include author-local drives and extraction code uses historical pandas APIs. [Database builder](https://github.com/sophie63/Aimon2022/blob/944c8367d5eed1a4ef477c702e31f31997762d34/MakeDBOfExperiements.ipynb), [region builder](https://github.com/sophie63/Aimon2022/blob/944c8367d5eed1a4ef477c702e31f31997762d34/MakeDBFunctionalRegions.ipynb).

A bounded in-memory download inspected only two numeric `.npy` files with `allow_pickle=False`: `CompTrialsAllFreq.npy`, 193,080 bytes, float64 `(271, 89)`; `FuncRPANTrials.npy`, 52,328 bytes, float64 `(87, 75)`. Their dimensions alone do **not** identify raw time/animal axes. GitHub tree metadata lists `GoodICsdf.pkl` at 240,944 bytes and `Regionsdf.pkl` at 8,738,870 bytes; they were not downloaded or unpickled. Do not turn figure summary rows into independent trial samples. This is a feasible metadata-first import, with raw trace coverage still to audit.

**Descending-neuron data:** `dn_experiments/loaddata.py` loads per-trial `processed/beh_df.pkl` and uses forward-velocity fields such as `v_forw`. Pairing requires the imaging table and acquisition synchronization, not joining on equal row number. The README distinguishes population imaging, headless behavior and supplementary datasets: do not merge them into fictitious simultaneous whole-animal records. [Pinned loader](https://github.com/NeLy-EPFL/dn_networks/blob/b9e66203ab825187e5d9bd651a63e46c2d5f0ab0/dn_experiments/loaddata.py).

**Small ORN pilot:** the non-formatted Figure 2 workbook is listed at 35.32 KB. The card documents fly/ROI identity, odor dilution, peak fluorescence, spike rate, and calcium kinetics; Figure 4 includes age groups and mean traces. Use genuinely paired measurements rather than matching similarly named fly columns across unrelated experiments. Group identifiers can be local to each sheet. Mean traces cannot support animal-held-out time-series evaluation. [Dataset schema](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.9p8cz8x0j).

## Proposed benchmarks, not completed experiments

**A. Real cross-modal calibration before language alignment.** Start with the small ORN workbooks. Predict measured peak spike response from calcium, concentration and allowed acquisition covariates; compare linear calibration, a small nonlinear model, and an embedding-conditioned model. Split by biological fly with all ROIs/concentrations/repeats together; choose hyperparameters within training flies and leave a different fly for validation. Audit whether fly IDs persist across sheets before pooling. Report per-fly MAE and correlation with uncertainty over flies. Exclude published regression coefficients, p-values, aggregate means and normalized test maxima from input features. This tests a measurement bridge, not behavior or thoughts. With only a few flies, use nested leave-one-fly-out and modest capacity rather than full encoder fine-tuning.

**B. Real neural↔behavior temporal bridge.** Prefer Aimon regional traces if time alignment and fly IDs are recoverable; otherwise use the DN population-imaging subset with its corresponding behavior tables. Train activity-window→behavior-state/velocity and the reverse behavior/stimulus→activity predictor. Use leave-one-animal-out outer tests, separate acquisition sessions for inner validation, and purge windows around temporal boundaries by at least the maximum model context plus the fitted calcium-response support. Fit normalization, region alignment, embeddings and calcium kernels on training data only. Never randomly split adjacent frames or trials from the same fly across the animal-held-out boundary. Keep spontaneous versus forced/optogenetic conditions as a held-out regime test. Compare neural-only, behavior-history-only, stimulus-only and shuffled-pair controls; metadata can otherwise reveal the answer. Behavioral captions must be generated from allowed observed variables, never the activity target being predicted. If cross-fly neuron identity is unavailable, use declared region/type pooling and missing-channel masks, not fabricated one-to-one neuron matching.

**C. FlyVis/FlyGym simulation benchmark for causal engineering.** Generate bounded movies (moving edges/blocks, varying contrast/speed) and FlyVis responses; optionally generate separate FlyGym trajectories with sensory input, motor command, pose and timestamps. Split by entire scene/trajectory seed and stimulus family, not overlapping windows. Hold out speed/contrast combinations and, separately, model/arena parameters; these are simulated-domain shifts, not animal generalization. Compare learned resampler/prefix with equal-budget linear and pooled models for stimulus-embedding↔activity reconstruction. Use stimulus captions from physical parameters, with those parameters removed from the neural-only decoder. Close the loop only when controller actions actually affect the simulator; score task success and interventions separately from embedding retrieval. Pin simulator version, timestep, render configuration, checkpoint and reset state. MPS/CUDA/CPU feasibility and throughput remain untested here.
